Model parameters Every rate is a model assumption; change it to test a hypothesis (saved with the run).
Grid 2160×1080 (1/6°, ~18 km). Present climate: Köppen-Geiger, Rubel et al. 2016. Elevation: ETOPO 10′. Orbit: Laskar et al. 2004. Ice volume: LR04 stack (Lisiecki & Raymo 2005), sea level −120 m × ice volume. Rivers, lakes, peaks: Natural Earth 10m. Volcanoes: plotly/datasets volcano list; eruption rates per VEI from the Wikipedia VEI table. People: start 1 places the known human range of the start year (Africa from 300 ka, Eurasia 55 ka, Sahul 50 ka, North America 21 ka, South America 14 ka; land not reachable without boats stays empty); start 2 puts people in Africa only. Lakes: the rim cells of rasterised lakes are returned to land, so lakes are drawn smaller. The north-east Pacific coast keeps a sea lane along the Cordilleran ice and coast-hugging boats move fast there (the coastal migration route). Groups spread over land and across water gaps of up to ~100 km; ice blocks them, colder winters need slow adaptation, land held by archaic humans (before 40 ka) is entered slowly. Foragers may settle in villages; settled villages may invent farming, most often in the known crop homelands (Larson et al. 2014) and only in a warm interglacial; farming spreads ~1 km/yr and can hop gaps of barren land or sea up to ~220 km. Farming groups become AI tribes; culture points grow with people, crowding and barriers; a chief makes a chiefdom; plateaus (×0.5), steppe (×0.5), jungle (×1/3) and plentiful wild game (×1/1.5, very rich ×1/3) lower the civ odds, the tsetse belt (humid tropical Africa: no draught animals or manure) ×1/3, a nearby settled civilization ×1.5 (secondary state formation), and sub-Saharan Africa has an adjustable factor (default ×0.5); a civilization needs its own faith, a ruler above the chiefs and 20,000+ people, then the civ odds decide when. Culture choices read only land and personality, never technology (technology changes capacity, army, trade and governance only). Multipliers and rates are model assumptions, not measurements; change them to test hypotheses. Civilizations: a ruler and a court of officials (skills 0–10); governance = ruler + officials, and land beyond what it can run (overstretch) costs amenity, army and expansion. Cities hold provinces; amenity (food, distance from the capital, overstretch, trade, war weariness, conquest, foreign people, ash, customs) and the ruler's skill set the odds of revolt; rebels fight for independence and old peoples revive their names. Weak rulers over restless cities can be overthrown by their best official (about a third of pre-modern reigns ended by deposition, Wang 2018). Opinion between civilizations in contact drifts with faith, kinship, borders, trade and old wars; trade opens between friendly partners with different goods; war odds rise with relative power, hostility, aggression and land hunger. The finer speeds (10 yr/s to 1 month/s) open once the first civilization forms. The run saves itself in this browser every 2 minutes and when the page is hidden; reopening resumes it and catches up the time that passed while the page was closed or asleep. Technology: 81 techs in 13 branches, each learned in levels (I-III, or I-V for the costly ones; roads I-VI, cart roads I-VII, tanks I-II; level I gives 75% of the effect); late techs (gunpowder and firearms, plate armour, compass, sternpost rudder, standing army) cost 30 times more for every 5 techs a society is short of their need, so they open late; every tech known nearby but not here speeds research, the strongest factor in the model — textiles (spinning and weaving, indigo, madder and weld, mordants, shellfish purple, cotton, silk, tailored clothes; dyes decide which colours flags and clothes can use), warfare (supply trains, depots, standing army — wars on states without a shared border need the enemy within supply range: 250 km + 200 per supply level + roads, horses, depots, standing army — bronze armour, mail, plate armour, gunpowder, fire lance, cannon, matchlock), metals (native copper, smelting, goldworking, lost-wax casting, bronze, iron, steel), tools (polished stone, pottery, potter's wheel, glass, metal tools), farming (manuring, irrigation, shaduf, plough, terraces, iron ploughshare), herding (herding, pack donkeys, milk and wool, horse riding, camel caravans, chariot, mounted nomadism), building, seafaring (dugout canoe, plank boats, sail, star navigation, war galleys, trireme, sternpost rudder, magnetic compass), learning (calendar, mathematics, astronomy, medicine) and politics (writing, laws, bureaucracy, coinage). In Technology mode each civilization shows what it is researching now; its panel shows the whole tree (known, researching, can research, locked). Each society chooses what to research from its land and character; knowledge spreads cheaply to neighbours; techs change capacity, army, trade and governance, never faith, titles or customs. Calibration targets for first inventions: copper use ~10 ka and smelting ~7 ka (Belovode), irrigation ~6000 BCE (Choga Mami), plough before 5000 BCE, bronze ~3300 BCE, horse riding ~3500 BCE (Botai), chariot ~2100–1700 BCE (Sintashta), iron ~1200 BCE, coinage 7th century BCE (Lydia); the simulated world starts farming ~1.5 kyr late, so dates run somewhat late. Political systems (monarchy, theocracy, military despotism, aristocratic oligarchy, republic, bureaucratic empire) are proposed by the ruler from his leanings and the state's character and pass only with enough support; very low support brings a revolution. Rulers and officials have four abilities (administration adds governance, military scales army power, diplomacy moves neighbours' opinion, learning speeds research); click a name in the panel for the ability window and the earlier rulers. Names of societies, people and places come from sound patterns of the language family of their region (invented words, not real names); a daughter society founded in another region usually takes up the local speech. Coastal civilizations with the sail found colonies across the sea (sail 500 km, star navigation 1,500 km, compass 5,000 km); colonists keep the mother country's speech and faith. The History panel charts the run every 50 years and exports it, and the current societies, as CSV; Model parameters lets you change the main rates and saves them with the run. Civilizations take in neighbouring chiefdoms by war or by cultural pull (shared god, kinship, a lead in knowledge). Hovering shows the land only; click a society for everything about it. Only its three main landmarks are drawn. 3D detail (Landforms checkbox, Natural, Development, Societies and Unrest maps): zoom in to see landform models (mesas, buttes, canyons, dunes, karst, fjords, deltas, peaks, volcanoes), rock peaks on bare mountains and trees by climate; cities appear as houses in their culture's material and roof form, capitals with their monument. Double-click zooms in. Click a society to see its ruler, officials, government, technology and buildings; click a building to draw it in its culture's style (material from technology, roof, monument form and motif from land and faith). Economy and roads (v15): a state taxes its people (low, normal or high, chosen by the ruler from needs and calm; high taxes anger cities) and trade; roads, cart roads, canals (from roads III), railways and motor roads raise the take. The treasury pays the army, building upkeep, new buildings and walls, and roads. Roads follow the cheapest path from the capital to every city over the state's own land; the Roads map shows them. Debt halves army regrowth, unsettles cities and raises coup odds, as do high taxes over unhappy cities. Every office holder has Sociability: the ruler picks officials by skill, sociability and how well their leanings match his own, the most influential (skill and sociability) take power in coups, and sociable rulers improve relations and trade talks. Heirs are sometimes inept (8%, a model choice). Trade partners share their techs (cheaper, as if known nearby) and trade raises income and research. Research on costly techs suffers random setbacks (10–35% of the progress, about 4% a year). A historical pace floor (model choice, can be switched off) keeps the leading civilization on a schedule that completes the tree by about 100 BP (from 600 BP its research is scaled to the points still needed), the eight largest to 70% of it, and pushes large states without iron onto the iron path from 1500 BP. Trade partners and the leaders may skip land conditions for techs after 2000 BP. Civilization odds: the nearest forming and the nearest settled state within 900 km count once each; river valleys build faith and authority faster; the best land a society holds counts at half. States and diplomacy (v16): wars need a shared border; allies (opinion 55+) join a defensive war without one, and such wars end without land changing hands. The attacker's logistics (supply trains, depots, standing army, cart roads, railways, motor roads) scale its strength from ×0.75 to ×1.35. A ready army and dominance over the neighbours lower revolt odds. An emperor (a bureaucratic empire, or a high king or divine ruler over 400+ cells) quarrels with other emperors and can take weaker neighbours (power ×2.5) as tributaries: they pay 8% of their income, are not attacked and trade more easily. Each political system has its own governance, army and economy multipliers; the people are pastoral nomads, semi-nomads or settled farmers by the land at the capital (dry land without rivers, herds and mounts), which changes army, governance, economy and building. Techs come in five tiers by cost, and 60% of the previous tier must be known to start the next (like Minecraft tool tiers); any tech whose prerequisites are met can also be found by luck (1 in 40,000 per year for a state, less for costly ones; tiers and late-tech gates still apply). The pace floor now aims at a complete tree by about 120 BP. Mediterranean coastal chiefdoms (Levant, Aegean) form city-states with half the people, and societies within 400 km of a Mediterranean-climate coast get ×3 odds (the trading world of the Levant and the Aegean, a model choice); highland penalties fade from 4000 to 1000 BP, not in cold lands. Early colonies come with the sail; chartered company colonies (merchant houses and compass) take up to 60 cells up to 9,000 km away; global companies (banking plus an industrial tech) open branches in trade partners and raise income. Territories use plain colours; the Flags box shows flag icons and flag-painted land. Peoples and rulers (v17): states settle ownerless land next to them at a steady rate (0.08 cells a year, more when developed), even poor land. Chiefdoms with a faith can unite two or more neighbouring chiefdoms of the same god, kin or speech into a tribal confederation (a state with its own system). When a ruler dies, the throne may pass to the ruler of an allied or kin state of the same faith (personal union, 6%), until the senior throne changes hands. At very low support (under 25) the most powerful official can usurp the throne. States can convert to the faith of a stronger, trading or overlord neighbour or of the people they rule, and take up neighbours' customs; people of the rulers' faith revolt at 30% of the usual odds, kin or similar peoples at 60%. Research on a tech leans on a resource (metals, coal, stone, timber, coast, rivers, steppe): ×0.75 without it up to ×1.25 with plenty, and trade partners that have it make up for a lack. Steam engines and electricity raise the economy (5% and 7% per level). Great people (scholar, general, statesman, merchant, engineer; levels I–V, at most 3 at once, 20–45 years) appear more often in big states and up to 3.1 times as often with writing, calendar, mathematics, astronomy, medicine, law and bureaucracy; each level adds 10% research, 8% army, 0.3 amenity, 6% income or 10% building. Desert floodplains (Nile, Euphrates, Indus) feed more people, chiefdoms near a settled state build faith and authority 2.5 times faster (emulation, secondary state formation), Mediterranean city-states need less authority for a king, river-valley societies in dry lands (Egyptian nomes, Sumerian city-states) need half the people for a state, and a chiefdom near a settled state may take up its faith. Egypt and North Africa have an adjustable factor (default ×3) on faith, authority and state odds, with the people needed for a state divided by its square root (a model choice like the sub-Saharan factor); states absorb distant chiefdoms less (× e^(−km/1000) from their capital). Flags now also use heraldic ordinaries (quarterly, bend, chevron, chequy, cross, saltire, fess, canton, roundel) and charges (eagle, lion, serpent, wheel, tower, horse, ship, sword, fish, sheaf), with metal charges on coloured fields and coloured charges on metal (the tincture rule). Research and peace (v18): societies first open new techs at level I before raising old ones, three times as eagerly for roads, cart roads, textiles and learning (gunpowder weapons, plate armour and the industrial tier are not pushed; industrial techs now need 66–76 distinct techs known and 80% of all tech levels). Every tech level learned pays off: support +1, a dividend of 0.3 years of income, and +0.4% income per distinct tech known (up to +40%). States claim unsettled land faster (0.25 cells a year) and further (1.5 × their reach), including canyons and wetlands; only high peaks stay out. The North African Mediterranean coast gets a further ×2 (state odds, faith and authority). China (18–45° N, 98–125° E) has adjustable slowdowns: civilization odds, faith and authority ×0.4 and research ×0.75 (model choices). A state whose people fall under 80% of their recent peak loses the will to start wars (× (people / peak)^4), and a war in which either side has lost a quarter of its people since it began ends in a negotiated peace (about half the time each year); when the people of all states fall under 90% of their peak of the last centuries, every war odds drop as well (× (people / peak)^6), and a state with fewer than 60% of the people its land can feed rebuilds before it fights (× (share / 0.6)^2). Interface and peoples (v19): the map has a side panel with Rankings (overall strength = 35% military power, 25% income, 15% people, 10% territory, 15% tech levels, each against the leader; or rank by one of them; click a row to open the society and centre the map), Society and Legend. The Flags box paints each civilization's flag on its land, with an opacity slider. The society panel shows the army's make-up from the techs it knows and its way of life (levies, bronze, iron or armoured infantry, musketeers, archers, cavalry, horse archers, chariots, camel riders, artillery, tanks, marines, ships). Seafaring peoples (like the Norse) arise where boats are known and the land is islands, or the capital is on the coast and an eighth of the land is coast (or a small state of up to 400 cells has 12+ coastal cells and little farmland); settled coastal peoples may turn to the sea. Each way of life favours its own research (seafarers: seafaring ×3 in choice and ×1.5 in speed, trade; nomads: herding and warfare; settled: farming, building, learning, politics); nomads and seafarers fight harder (army ×1.35 and ×1.25) but research tier 3–4 techs slower (×0.75 and ×0.85). Nomads, semi-nomads and seafarers settle over time when they hold farmland or live among settled trading neighbours; settled peoples do not go back to herding. New political ideas (constitutionalism, nationalism, representative democracy, anarchism, socialism) open constitutional monarchy (amenity +1, economy ×1.1), democracy (amenity +1.5, economy ×1.25, research ×1.1, army ×0.9), the communist state (governance ×1.3, army ×1.2, research ×1.1, amenity −0.5) and the anarchist commune (amenity +2, governance ×0.5, army ×0.7); nationalism calms the state's own people (+1) and angers others (−1) and adds 10% to armies. Speed (v20): a climate update redoes only what changed. Each worker keeps the last era's neighbourhood sums (fertile land, domestic animals, ice margin, natural barriers), sea distances, resources and per-cell odds, and updates them only around the cells whose climate changed (a few thousand per era step); after a large jump it recomputes everything. The map image is repainted only where climate, summer temperature or relief changed, and while time runs the workers compute the next era in advance. Results are the same as before (odds differ by less than one part in ten million, from rounding). Americas (v20.1): state formation is slower in the Americas (realms of North and South America): faith, authority and state odds x0.5, and settling and farming x0.5 (both adjustable). A model choice: a small founding population crossed Beringia (genetic estimates of a few hundred people) and there were fewer competing neighbours; the first states there arose late (Monte Alban in Oaxaca, 300-100 BC). Flags: pre-modern banners (plain field, bordered, chief, per pale, pennant, swallowtail, hanging vexillum; no modern tricolours) in natural dye colours: undyed white, charcoal black, ochre red and yellow and earth brown at first, then madder (orange, maroon), indigo (blue), mordants (green) and shellfish purple as the society learns them. Colours follow climate and character, the emblem a landmark (highest peak, volcano, sea, sun faith, dominant animal); a new government or a new dye may bring a new flag. Each civilization's territory is painted with its flag. Technology only limits the colours; it chooses nothing. Nature: earthquakes and eruptions are off at 100 yr/s or faster and happen at 1/10 of the observed rates (a model choice). Earthquakes near plate boundaries (PB2002, Bird 2003) by Mercalli intensity — VI–VIII damage harvests and buildings for 5 years, IX for 10, X extremely rarely, XI–XII never; minor intensity V shocks only at 1 month/s; earthquakes are not marked or logged at 2 yr/s or faster; the Hazards checkbox hides all hazard markers; eruptions VEI 4–6 hit the area for 3–8 years, VEI 7–8 also bring a 10-year volcanic winter; tropical cyclones (only at 1 yr/s or slower) last 1 month to a year by category and follow ENSO; El Niño and La Niña (only at 1 yr/s or slower) bring yearly famine or plenty to the regions they reach, with month-by-month detail at 6 months/s and 1 month/s. Borders are thin vector lines. When cells are larger than 2.5 screen pixels the map is redrawn per screen pixel in a background worker, like a tiled web map: colours are interpolated between cells, coasts and territory edges follow smoothed outlines, borders are drawn at pixel level, and relief adds procedural sub-cell roughness (by terrain class: flat 20 m, hills 250 m, mountains 800 m) to the ETOPO heights. Detail finer than one cell (about 18 km) is therefore illustrative, not data. While panning, the previous image is moved until the new one is ready. A new climate era is computed only when ice volume or 65°N June insolation has moved enough; time keeps running on the last climate for up to three era steps. The climate, resources, odds and relief of each era are computed in parallel by Web Workers (one per spare CPU core, falling back to the main thread).
